# Japanese report style

Use these defaults where an approved template or house style does not already decide the matter.

Write for a Japanese manager who needs to understand a finding and its consequence. Prefer direct, neutral sentences with concrete subjects and verbs. Default to consistent `である` style; retain approved `です・ます` style when supplied. Avoid unnecessary honorifics, sales language, emotional emphasis, and vague consulting expressions.

Keep the source's section structure and level of detail. Improve sentences within it. Do not invent a title, summary, rationale, action plan, or decision to make the document feel complete. Preserve repetition that serves a distinct purpose, such as a condition repeated in both summary and detailed recommendation.

Put an already stated conclusion early within its paragraph when helpful without changing emphasis or logic. Keep material conditions with it. A descriptive heading is safer than an unqualified conclusion when a short heading cannot retain its condition.

## Sentence editing

- Prefer verbs over chains of abstract nouns when the intended action is clear.
- Use established Japanese terminology instead of word-for-word English metaphors.
- Clarify grammatical relationships using the source's actors and referents; do not guess an omitted actor.
- Split long sentences at genuine claim boundaries. Retain the original causal, adversative, or conditional relationship; do not add one.
- Replace vague words with concrete referents only when the source uniquely supplies them.
- Avoid scattering `〜と考えられる`, `〜と思われる`, or `〜と言える` everywhere. Match each source claim's actual certainty rather than applying a global assertiveness rule.
- Repeat defined terms consistently instead of varying them for style.

Naturalness and brevity are secondary to meaning. A longer sentence retaining its actor and condition is preferable to a shorter ambiguous one.

## Typography and terminology

Use Japanese punctuation `、。` in prose and generally half-width Arabic numerals and Latin abbreviations. Preserve an existing consistent template. Choose consistent date/number notation without changing values or precision. Keep official capitalization, rating codes, currency identifiers, source IDs, URLs, placeholders, and formulas.

Do not automatically expand abbreviations or convert them all to katakana. Explain a technical term only when the source or approved glossary supplies the explanation. Preserve distinctions between data, interpretation, and recommendation through ordinary Japanese wording.

## natural-japanese integration

The vendored guide provides deeper editing guidance and local lint helpers. Its examples are not universal transformations. Before deleting an allegedly exaggerated word, check whether it carries a genuine quantifier or judgment. `例外なく` may be the claim itself; `未確認` may be a decision-critical limitation.

Do not optimize for an AI-detection or naturalness score. Check whether the reader identifies the same conclusion, evidence, limitations, and next step as in the source.
